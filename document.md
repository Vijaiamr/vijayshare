# Chart Patterns & Candlestick Patterns — Complete Reference

> Reliability percentages are based on research by Thomas Bulkowski (Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns, 3rd Ed.), Steve Nison (Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques), and statistical backtests on equities markets. Actual performance varies by timeframe, market, and conditions.

---

# PART 1: CHART PATTERNS

---

## A. REVERSAL PATTERNS

---

### 1. Head and Shoulders (Top)

**Type:** Major Reversal (Bullish → Bearish)

**How it works:**
An uptrend stalls, forms a higher peak (head) flanked by two lower peaks (shoulders). A neckline connects the troughs. Price breaks below the neckline, confirming the reversal. The measured target is the distance from head to neckline, projected downward from the break.

**Identification:**
- Prior uptrend must exist
- Left shoulder: rally, pullback to neckline
- Head: higher rally above left shoulder, pullback to neckline
- Right shoulder: weaker rally, lower than head
- Volume: highest on left shoulder, lower on head, lowest on right shoulder
- Neckline: support connecting the two troughs — can slope up, down, or flat
- Break: close below neckline (ideal with volume expansion)

**Why it is believed to work:**
- The pattern represents exhaustion of buyers
- The final failed attempt to push higher (right shoulder) shows diminishing momentum
- Neckline break acts as trapped bulls becoming sellers
- Historically one of the most reliable reversal patterns across all timeframes

**Psychology:**
- **Left shoulder:** Bulls still in control, buying dips
- **Head:** Euphoria — last wave of optimism, late buyers FOMO in
- **Right shoulder:** Smart money distributes to latecomers; enthusiasm wanes; each rally is weaker
- **Neckline break:** Fear sets in; those who bought at the top panic; selling accelerates

**Reliability:** ~85% reversal rate when confirmed with volume. Average decline: 20%. Failure rate ~15% (often due to premature identification or weak neckline break).

---

### 2. Inverse Head and Shoulders (Bottom)

**Type:** Major Reversal (Bearish → Bullish)

**How it works:**
A downtrend reverses. Three troughs form: a lower middle trough (head) flanked by two higher troughs (shoulders). A neckline connects the peaks. Price breaks above the neckline, confirming reversal. Measured target = head-to-neckline distance projected upward.

**Identification:**
- Prior downtrend required
- Left shoulder: decline, bounce to neckline
- Head: lower low, bounce to neckline
- Right shoulder: higher low (fails to match the low of head), bounce
- Volume: heaviest on head or right shoulder (accumulation)
- Break: close above neckline with increasing volume

**Why it is believed to work:**
- The pattern shows sellers exhausting themselves
- The head represents a final capitulation
- Higher lows on the right shoulder indicate accumulation by smart money
- Break above neckline confirms the shift in control

**Psychology:**
- **Left shoulder:** Bears dominant, but some buying emerges
- **Head:** Maximum pessimism — capitulation, weak holders sell in despair
- **Right shoulder:** "I'm not selling anymore" — sellers drying up; informed buyers accumulating
- **Neckline break:** Regret and FOMO — those who sold at the bottom buy back; new bulls enter

**Reliability:** ~87% reversal rate. Average gain: 25%. More reliable on weekly/monthly timeframes.

---

### 3. Double Top

**Type:** Reversal (Bullish → Bearish)

**How it works:**
Price rallies to a resistance level twice, fails to break higher, then falls below the intervening trough (support). The pattern resembles the letter "M".

**Identification:**
- Prior uptrend
- Two peaks at approximately the same price level (±3%)
- A trough between them (the valley)
- Volume: first peak usually higher volume than second
- Break below the valley confirms the pattern
- Measured move = height of pattern projected downward from break level

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Two failed attempts to break resistance confirm strong selling at that level
- Break of the valley means previous support becomes resistance
- The pattern traps breakout buyers who bought the second peak

**Psychology:**
- **First peak:** Buyers push price up, sellers reject it
- **Trough:** Some buying returns
- **Second peak:** Late bulls try again but fail at same level; early holders exit; "double top" anticipation
- **Break below valley:** Trapped longs panic; bears pile on

**Reliability:** ~75-80% if confirmed. False breakouts above the second peak are common (10-15%). Average decline: 15%.

---

### 4. Double Bottom

**Type:** Reversal (Bearish → Bullish)

**How it works:**
Price declines to a support level twice, fails to break lower, then rallies above the intervening peak. Resembles the letter "W".

**Identification:**
- Prior downtrend
- Two troughs at approximately the same price level (±3%)
- A peak between them
- Volume: often higher on the second bottom (capitulation/accumulation)
- Break above the peak confirms
- Measured move = height projected upward from break

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Two failed attempts to break support indicate strong buying at that level
- Break of the intervening peak traps short sellers
- Accumulation by informed traders

**Psychology:**
- **First bottom:** Bears push down, buyers step in
- **Rally:** Short covering, some hope
- **Second bottom:** Those who didn't buy the first time get a second chance; sellers exhausted; volume spikes
- **Break above peak:** Shorts trapped, FOMO buyers enter

**Reliability:** ~80%. False breakdowns below the second bottom happen ~10%. Average gain: 20%.

---

### 5. Triple Top

**Type:** Reversal (Bullish → Bearish)

**How it works:**
Price hits the same resistance level three times, each with diminishing momentum, then breaks below support.

**Identification:**
- Prior uptrend
- Three roughly equal peaks
- Two intervening troughs at similar levels (support)
- Volume decreasing with each peak
- Break below the support level confirmed

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Three rejections at the same level confirm it as strong resistance
- Each rally has less force — the third peak is the final gasp
- Multiple trapped buyers at each peak

**Psychology:**
- **1st peak:** Rejected
- **2nd peak:** "Let me try again" — still rejected
- **3rd peak:** Desperation — the weakest rally, late buyers who "missed" the first two
- **Breakdown:** Maximum pain for all three groups of trapped buyers

**Reliability:** ~80-85%. Average decline: 18%. The volume pattern is critical for confirmation.

---

### 6. Triple Bottom

**Type:** Reversal (Bearish → Bullish)

**How it works:**
Price hits the same support level three times, each time bouncing higher, then breaks above resistance.

**Identification:**
- Prior downtrend
- Three roughly equal troughs
- Two intervening peaks at similar levels
- Volume often increases on the third bottom (accumulation)
- Break above resistance confirms

**Psychology:**
- **1st bottom:** Bought by value hunters
- **2nd bottom:** "Was that it?" — second test holds
- **3rd bottom:** "That's definitely it" — everyone who wanted to sell has sold; smart money loads up
- **Breakout:** Shorts trapped; general relief and buying

**Reliability:** ~82%. Average gain: 22%.

---

### 7. Rounding Top (Dome)

**Type:** Reversal (Bullish → Bearish)

**How it works:**
A gradual, curved transition from uptrend to downtrend. Price moves up, stalls, plateaus, and slowly declines, forming a dome shape.

**Identification:**
- Extended uptrend preceding
- Gradual slowdown (not sharp reversal)
- Shape is a smooth arc (weeks to months)
- Volume: high during uptrend, declining during plateau, increasing on the downside

**Psychology:**
- A slow, consensus-driven shift
- No sudden panic — just gradual loss of enthusiasm
- Bulls slowly realize the trend is over
- Accumulation distribution by institutions over time

**Reliability:** ~75-80% but hard to identify early. Average decline: 14%.

---

### 8. Rounding Bottom (Saucer)

**Type:** Reversal (Bearish → Bullish)

**How it works:**
A slow, curved transition from downtrend to uptrend. Price gradually stops declining, moves sideways, then slowly begins rising.

**Identification:**
- Extended downtrend prior
- Smooth U-shaped bottom (weeks to months)
- Volume: contracting on the left, very low at the bottom, expanding on the right side

**Psychology:**
- Slow accumulation by institutions
- General apathy at the bottom — "nobody cares anymore"
- Gradual improvement in sentiment without euphoria
- The most sustainable type of bottom

**Reliability:** ~80%. Average gain: 30-40%, but takes much longer to play out.

---

### 9. V-Shaped Reversal (Spike Top / Spike Bottom)

**Type:** Reversal (Bullish → Bearish / Bearish → Bullish)

**How it works:**
A sharp, sudden reversal with no transition. Price goes straight up then straight down (V-top) or straight down then straight up (V-bottom).

**Identification:**
- Steep, near-vertical move preceding
- No consolidation or rounding
- Reversal is equally sharp
- Often with huge volume on the turn
- Usually driven by a catalyst

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Represents emotional extremes
- Exhaustion of the prevailing move forces a snap-back
- Often triggered specifically by news/events

**Psychology:**
- **V-Top:** Parabolic buying → everyone is in → no more buyers → sudden collapse when the last bull buys
- **V-Bottom:** Panic selling → everyone who wanted to sell has sold → forced liquidation ends → explosion higher
- Pure fear and greed extremes

**Reliability:** Low predictability (~55-65%) because they lack structure. Near-impossible to trade in real time without confirmation.

---

### 10. Island Reversal

**Type:** Reversal (at peaks and troughs)

**How it works:**
A price gap away from the prevailing trend, a few days/weeks of trading in isolation, then another gap in the opposite direction, leaving the "island" isolated.

**Identification:**
- Gap up/down to create the island
- Several sessions of trading in a range
- Gap back the other way to close the island
- Often with high volume on both gaps

**Why it is believed to work:**
- The gaps represent a sudden, complete change in market opinion
- Everyone who traded during the island is trapped (either all buyers or all sellers)

**Psychology:**
- **First gap:** Shock — price jumps away from all prior levels
- **Island:** Confusion, uncertainty, low volume
- **Second gap:** Realization — the first gap was wrong; trapped traders unwind positions
- Pure emotional whipsaw

**Reliability:** ~70-75%. Rare pattern but powerful when it appears correctly.

## B. CONTINUATION PATTERNS

---

### 11. Flags (Bull Flag / Bear Flag)

**Type:** Continuation

**How it works:**
A sharp directional move (flagpole) followed by a small counter-trend consolidation channel (flag). Price then breaks out in the original direction.

**Identification:**
- Strong, near-vertical move (pole)
- Consolidation drifts opposite to the pole (flag) — parallel channel
- Flag is shorter than the pole
- Volume: high on pole, declining during flag, spiking on breakout
- Breakout in the same direction as the pole

**Why it is believed to work:**
- The flag represents a pause for profit-taking before the next leg
- The counter-trend drift shakes out weak hands
- Institutions use the pause to accumulate/ distribute more

**Psychology:**
- **Pole:** Momentum — FOMO, aggressive buying/selling
- **Flag:** "Should I stay or should I go?" — doubt creeps in; weak holders exit
- **Breakout:** "It's still going!" — fresh conviction, more volume, continuation

**Reliability:** ~80-85%. Measured move = flagpole height projected from breakout. One of the most reliable patterns.

---

### 12. Pennants (Bull Pennant / Bear Pennant)

**Type:** Continuation

**How it works:**
Similar to flags but the consolidation is a symmetrical triangle (converging lines) rather than a parallel channel.

**Identification:**
- Sharp prior move (pole)
- Consolidation forms converging trendlines (triangle shape)
- Volume contracts during pennant, expands on breakout
- Breakout resumes the prior trend

**Psychology:**
- The converging lines indicate the consolidation is tightening
- Traders are waiting for a resolution
- The breakout direction is explosive when it comes

**Reliability:** ~80%. Similar to flags. The tighter the pennant, the more explosive the breakout.

---

### 13. Wedges (Rising Wedge / Falling Wedge)

**Type:** Rising Wedge ≈ Bearish (or continuation in downtrend)
        Falling Wedge ≈ Bullish (or continuation in uptrend)

**How it works:**
Price moves between two converging trendlines that both slope in the same direction.

**Rising Wedge:** Both lines slope up, but the lower line is steeper. Price eventually breaks down.
**Falling Wedge:** Both lines slope down, but the upper line is steeper. Price eventually breaks up.

**Identification:**
- Converging trendlines (not parallel)
- Both slope same direction
- Declining volume during formation
- Breakout opposite to the wedge slope (usually)

**Why it is believed to work:**
- In a rising wedge, each rally is weaker — momentum is stalling
- In a falling wedge, each decline is weaker — sellers are running out

**Psychology:**
- **Rising Wedge:** Lower-highs pattern in disguise; shows diminishing buyer enthusiasm
- **Falling Wedge:** Higher-lows pattern in disguise; shows diminishing seller pressure

**Reliability:** ~75-78% for falling wedge (bullish), ~70-75% for rising wedge (bearish). Falling wedge is more reliable.

---

### 14. Rectangles (Trading Range / Consolidation)

**Type:** Continuation (can also be reversal)

**How it works:**
Price moves between parallel horizontal support and resistance levels. Breakout typically continues the prior trend.

**Identification:**
- Prior trend established
- At least two touches of both support and resistance
- Horizontal, parallel lines
- Volume declining during range, increasing on breakout
- Breakout resumes prior trend ~75% of the time

**Psychology:**
- A tug-of-war between buyers and sellers at a defined price zone
- Each side tests the boundary
- The trend prior to the rectangle tells you which side eventually wins

**Reliability:** ~75% as continuation. Fairly reliable but can sometimes become a reversal top/bottom.

---

### 15. Ascending Triangle

**Type:** Continuation (Bullish)

**How it works:**
A flat horizontal resistance line and a rising support line (higher lows). Price eventually breaks through resistance.

**Identification:**
- Horizontal resistance (at least 2 touches)
- Rising support (higher lows — at least 2 touches)
- Prior uptrend is ideal
- Volume: declining during formation, spiking on breakout
- Measured target = height of triangle added to breakout level

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Buyers are more aggressive with each pullback (higher lows)
- Sellers maintain a hard line at resistance
- Eventually the buying pressure overwhelms the sellers

**Psychology:**
- Each dip is bought earlier — shows increasing urgency
- The resistance line holds, but it's getting hammered more each time
- Smart money accumulates on each dip; retail waits for breakout

**Reliability:** ~82%. One of the more reliable continuation patterns.

---

### 16. Descending Triangle

**Type:** Continuation (Bearish)

**How it works:**
A flat horizontal support line and a declining resistance line (lower highs). Price eventually breaks through support.

**Identification:**
- Horizontal support (at least 2 touches)
- Declining resistance (lower highs)
- Prior downtrend is ideal
- Volume often increases on breakdown

**Psychology:**
- Sellers are more aggressive with each rally
- Buyers defend a hard line but are getting weaker
- Eventually selling pressure overwhelms

**Reliability:** ~80%.

---

### 17. Symmetrical Triangle

**Type:** Bilateral (can break either way)

**How it works:**
Converging trendlines — lower highs and higher lows. Price compresses and eventually breaks out, typically in the direction of the prior trend.

**Identification:**
- Two converging trendlines (one down, one up)
- At least 4 touchpoints (2 on each side)
- Volume compresses significantly during formation
- Breakout with volume expansion

**Psychology:**
- Neutral indecision — both sides are uncertain
- The compression builds energy for an explosive move
- Prior trend gives the best clue for direction

**Reliability:** ~70-75% when breaking in the direction of the prior trend. ~55% when against trend.

---

### 18. Expanding Triangle (Broadening / Megaphone)

**Type:** Bilateral (high volatility pattern)

**How it works:**
Price makes higher highs and lower lows, forming a widening range. Opposite of a triangle.

**Identification:**
- At least 2 higher highs and 2 lower lows
- Diverging trendlines
- Often increasing volume
- Final break can be explosive in either direction

**Psychology:**
- Increasing disagreement between buyers and sellers
- Emotional, irrational market — "everyone has an opinion"
- Often marks a speculative top or panic bottom
- The wider the range, the more unstable

**Reliability:** ~55-65%. Difficult to trade. Higher failure rate. Better as a volatility indicator.

---

### 19. Cup and Handle

**Type:** Continuation (Bullish)

**How it works:**
A rounded bottom (cup) followed by a short pullback (handle), then a breakout to new highs.

**Identification:**
- Prior uptrend
- Cup: U-shaped rounded bottom (not V-shaped)
- Handle: small pullback forming a flag/pennant on the right side
- Cup depth: ideally no more than 50% of the prior move
- Breakout above handle resistance with volume
- Measured target = depth of cup added to breakout level

**Why it is believed to work:**
- The cup is a long consolidation that shakes out weak holders
- The handle is the final shakeout before the run resumes

**Psychology:**
- **Cup:** Gradual accumulation. The left side = selling, bottom = apathy, right side = renewed interest
- **Handle:** "I've been waiting for months and nothing's happening, I'm out" — the exact wrong time to leave
- **Breakout:** Fresh momentum, all trapped shorts and FOMO buyers push it higher

**Reliability:** ~80%. More reliable on longer timeframes and with shallower cups.

---

### 20. Inverted Cup and Handle

**Type:** Continuation (Bearish)

**How it works:**
Inverse of the cup and handle. A rounded top (inverted cup) followed by a small pullback up (handle), then breakdown.

**Psychology:** Opposite of cup and handle. Distribution rather than accumulation.

**Reliability:** ~75%. Less common and less reliable than the standard cup and handle.

---

### 21. Measured Move (Run)

**Type:** Continuation

**How it works:**
Three distinct legs: an initial move (leg 1), a corrective pullback (leg 2), then a resumption in the same direction (leg 3) that roughly equals leg 1 in length.

**Psychology:**
- **Leg 1:** Strong directional conviction
- **Leg 2:** Profit-taking; doubt; the crowd re-evaluates
- **Leg 3:** Confirmation — the trend is real; new participants join

**Reliability:** ~70-75%.

---

### 22. Gap Patterns

**How they work / Psychology / Reliability:**

| Gap Type | When | Psychology | Reliability |
|----------|------|------------|-------------|
| **Breakaway Gap** | Out of a consolidation/pattern | Sudden realization; new information; urgent entry | High — often starts new trends |
| **Runaway/Continuation Gap** | Middle of a strong trend | Momentum; FOMO; "I need in" | ~50% chance of another gap forming (measuring gap) |
| **Exhaustion Gap** | End of a trend, with high volume | Last gasp of the trend; trapped latecomers | Reversal follows 60-70% of the time |
| **Common Gap** | Low volume, no pattern significance | Random noise | Usually fills within days |
| **Gap Fill** | Price returns to fill prior gap | "Unfair" price must be re-tested | 90%+ of gaps eventually fill |

# PART 2: CANDLESTICK PATTERNS

---

## A. SINGLE CANDLE PATTERNS

---

### 1. Doji

**Type:** Indecision / Reversal

**How it works:**
Open and close at almost the same price, creating a thin body (or cross). Indicates that neither bulls nor bears won the session.

**Variants:**
- **Neutral Doji:** Small shadow — pure indecision
- **Long-Legged Doji (Rickshaw Man):** Long upper and lower shadows — extreme indecision, wide range but close in the middle
- **Dragonfly Doji:** Long lower shadow, no upper shadow — session opened high, sold off, then recovered to open
- **Gravestone Doji:** Long upper shadow, no lower shadow — session opened low, rallied, then fell back to open

**Identification:**
- Body length ≤ 5% of total candle range
- Shadows vary by type

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Represents a standoff where neither side can seize control
- After a strong trend, it signals that the trend has lost momentum
- Needs confirmation from the next candle

**Psychology:**
- **After uptrend:** Buyers pushed it up but sellers brought it back — first sign of weakness
- **After downtrend:** Sellers pushed it down but buyers brought it back — first sign of strength
- **Dragonfly:** Bulls rejected the lows (bullish signal)
- **Gravestone:** Bears rejected the highs (bearish signal)
- **Long-Legged:** Both sides fought hard and nobody won (significant indecision)

**Trust percentage:** 50-60% alone (needs confirmation). Dragonfly/Gravestone slightly higher (~65%). With next-candle confirmation ~75%.

---

### 2. Marubozu

**Type:** Continuation / Strength signal

**How it works:**
A candle with no (or very short) shadows. The entire range is the body.

**Bullish Marubozu (White/Green):** Open = low, close = high. Sellers never had a chance.
**Bearish Marubozu (Black/Red):** Open = high, close = low. Buyers never had a chance.

**Identification:**
- No upper or lower shadow (or negligible)
- Full-bodied candle

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Shows complete dominance by one side for the entire session
- Implies strong momentum will continue

**Psychology:**
- **Bullish:** "I want to buy and I don't care about the price" — aggressive buying from open to close
- **Bearish:** "I want to sell and I don't care about the price" — aggressive selling from open to close

**Trust percentage:** ~70-75% as continuation signal. Less reliable as reversal signal.

---

### 3. Spinning Top

**Type:** Indecision

**How it works:**
Small body (open ≈ close), but with upper and lower shadows longer than the body.

**Identification:**
- Body is small
- Upper and lower shadows are longer than the body

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Signals that neither bulls nor bears could maintain control
- During a trend, it suggests the trend is losing steam

**Psychology:**
Both sides pushed price around, but in the end it barely moved — confusion and uncertainty.

**Trust percentage:** ~55% alone. Important in context (e.g., after a long trend or at resistance/support).

---

### 4. Hammer

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
A small body at the upper end of the range, with a long lower shadow (at least 2-3x the body). Appears in a downtrend.

**Identification:**
- Prior downtrend
- Small body at the top of the candle
- Lower shadow ≥ 2x body length
- Little or no upper shadow
- Color of the body is less important (green is slightly stronger)

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Price was sold down hard during the session but bought back aggressively
- The market "rejected" the lower prices
- Shows that buyers stepped in and absorbed selling pressure

**Psychology:**
- Bears pushed price down (long shadow)
- By the close, bulls had bought it all back
- Message: "We tried to push it lower and couldn't keep it there"
- The downtrend is being challenged

**Trust percentage:** ~75% with confirmation (higher close next session). Green hammer ~78%, red hammer ~72%.

---

### 5. Hanging Man

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
Exactly the same shape as a hammer — small body, long lower shadow — but appears in an uptrend.

**Identification:**
- Prior uptrend
- Same shape as hammer
- Long lower shadow, small body at the top

**Why it is believed to work:**
- During an uptrend, a long lower shadow means sellers pushed price down significantly
- Although bulls recovered it by close, the fact that selling happened at all is a warning

**Psychology:**
- "Wait — people were selling aggressively today?"
- The long lower shadow shows sellers are active
- The recovery to the top is a trap for late bulls
- First warning of distribution

**Trust percentage:** ~70% with confirmation (lower close next session).

---

### 6. Inverted Hammer

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
Small body at the bottom of the range, long upper shadow. Appears in a downtrend.

**Identification:**
- Prior downtrend
- Small body at bottom
- Upper shadow ≥ 2x body
- Little or no lower shadow

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Buyers attempted a rally during the session (long upper shadow)
- Sellers brought it back down, but the attempt happened — buyers are testing
- Needs next-day confirmation

**Psychology:**
- "Someone tried to buy it up today" — the first attempt to start a rally
- Even though sellers won the session, the fact that buyers tried is new
- Shows possible accumulation starting

**Trust percentage:** ~70% with confirmation.

---

### 7. Shooting Star

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
Small body at the bottom of the range, long upper shadow. Appears in an uptrend.

**Identification:**
- Prior uptrend
- Small body at bottom
- Long upper shadow ≥ 2x body
- Little or no lower shadow
- Color unimportant, but red is slightly stronger

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Buyers pushed price up to new highs (long upper shadow)
- But sellers aggressively sold it back down
- Close near the low means the rally failed
- "Rejected at the top"

**Psychology:**
- Bulls pushed it up, expecting the uptrend to continue
- Sellers appeared at the high and overwhelmed them
- The close near the low is a defeat for bulls
- Classic topping signal

**Trust percentage:** ~75%. One of the more reliable single-candle reversal patterns.

---

### 8. Bullish Belt Hold

**Type:** Bullish Reversal / Continuation

**How it works:**
A white/green candle that opens at or near its low (no lower shadow) and rallies throughout the session.

**Identification:**
- Open = low (no lower shadow)
- Long white body
- May have a small upper shadow

**Why it is believed to work:**
- The open was the low — buyers never let it go below that level
- Aggressive buying from the first tick

**Psychology:**
"Starting right here, we're buying and not looking back." Decisive bullish entry.

**Trust percentage:** ~70%.

---

### 9. Bearish Belt Hold

**Type:** Bearish Reversal / Continuation

**How it works:**
A black/red candle that opens at or near its high and sells off the entire session.

**Identification:**
- Open = high (no upper shadow)
- Long black body

**Psychology:**
"From the open, we're selling." Complete bearish conviction.

**Trust percentage:** ~70%.

## B. TWO-CANDLE PATTERNS

---

### 10. Bullish Engulfing

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
A small bearish candle is followed by a larger bullish candle whose body completely "engulfs" the previous candle's body.

**Identification:**
- Prior downtrend
- First candle: small bearish (red/black)
- Second candle: larger bullish (green/white)
- Second candle's body fully covers the first candle's body (shadows may or may not be engulfed)
- Volume on the second candle is ideally higher

**Why it is believed to work:**
- The shift from bearish to bullish is emphatic
- The bullish candle is larger, showing momentum in the reversal direction
- Everyone who bought in the prior session is now profitable

**Psychology:**
- **Day 1:** Bears in control, but only weakly (small body)
- **Day 2:** Opens near prior close, then bulls take over completely
- All the bears who sold in the prior session are now trapped
- The larger body shows conviction, not hesitation

**Trust percentage:** ~80%. Higher with volume confirmation and when appearing at support levels.

---

### 11. Bearish Engulfing

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
A small bullish candle is followed by a larger bearish candle whose body engulfs the previous candle's body.

**Identification:**
- Prior uptrend
- First candle: small bullish
- Second candle: larger bearish that fully engulfs the first body
- Volume confirmation ideal

**Psychology:**
- **Day 1:** Bulls in control
- **Day 2:** Opens near prior close, then bears take over
- All the bulls from the prior session are now losing
- The larger body shows conviction to the downside

**Trust percentage:** ~80%.

---

### 12. Bullish Harami

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
A large bearish candle is followed by a small bullish candle whose body sits entirely within the previous body. (Reverse of engulfing.)

**Identification:**
- Prior downtrend
- First: large bearish candle
- Second: small bullish candle with body inside the first body
- The second can be a doji (Harami Cross)

**Why it is believed to work:**
- The selling pressure has diminished significantly
- The small body shows indecision after a strong move — the first sign of exhaustion

**Psychology:**
- **Day 1:** Strong selling
- **Day 2:** The selling stops abruptly — small range, close higher
- The bears failed to follow through; bulls sense opportunity
- "No more sellers" — the trend is exhausted

**Trust percentage:** ~65-70% (weaker than engulfing because no strong buying yet; needs confirmation).

---

### 13. Bearish Harami

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
A large bullish candle followed by a small bearish candle whose body sits inside the first body.

**Psychology:**
- **Day 1:** Strong buying
- **Day 2:** The buying stops; small range, close lower
- No follow-through from bulls
- Warning that the uptrend is losing steam

**Trust percentage:** ~65-70%.

---

### 14. Harami Cross (Bullish / Bearish)

**Type:** Reversal

**How it works:**
Same as harami, but the second candle is a doji (instead of a small body).

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Even stronger indecision than a regular harami
- After a strong move, a doji inside the prior body means the trend has completely stalled

**Trust percentage:** ~75% (stronger than regular harami).

---

### 15. Piercing Line

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
A bearish candle is followed by a bullish candle that opens lower but closes more than halfway up the prior bearish candle's body.

**Identification:**
- Prior downtrend
- First: bearish candle
- Second: bullish candle opens below first candle's low, closes above the midpoint of the first body
- Does NOT close above the first candle's open (that would be engulfing)

**Why it is believed to work:**
- The gap down at the open traps sellers
- The recovery and close past the midpoint shows that buyers absorbed all selling

**Psychology:**
- **Day 1:** Bears in control
- **Day 2 opens lower:** "Oh no, even lower" — despair
- **Day 2 closes:** Reversal of fortune — all the early sellers are losing
- The close above the midpoint is the key: buyers didn't just fight back, they won the session

**Trust percentage:** ~78%. Requires more than halfway — if it closes exactly at the midpoint or below, it's the weaker "In-Neck" pattern.

---

### 16. Dark Cloud Cover

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
A bullish candle is followed by a bearish candle that opens higher but closes more than halfway down the prior bullish body.

**Identification:**
- Prior uptrend
- First: strong bullish candle
- Second: bearish candle opens above the previous high, closes below the midpoint of the first body

**Psychology:**
- **Day 2 opens higher:** Euphoria — gap up
- **Day 2 closes:** Sold off completely; the bulls who bought at the open are trapped
- The "dark cloud" overshadows the previous optimism

**Trust percentage:** ~78%.

---

### 17. Tweezer Top

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
Two consecutive candles with the same high. First is typically bullish, second is bearish.

**Identification:**
- Prior uptrend
- Both highs are equal (or very close, within a few ticks)
- First candle: bullish
- Second candle: bearish

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Price hit the same level twice and was rejected both times
- The second rejection is more significant because bears also closed lower

**Psychology:**
"Price can't get past this level." Double rejection at resistance.

**Trust percentage:** ~70%.

---

### 18. Tweezer Bottom

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
Two consecutive candles with the same low. First bearish, second bullish.

**Psychology:**
"We can't break below this level." Double rejection at support.

**Trust percentage:** ~70%.

---

### 19. Meeting Lines (Bullish / Bearish)

**Type:** Reversal

**How it works:**
First candle trends strongly. Second candle opens with a gap, then closes exactly at or very near the close of the first candle.

**Bullish Meeting Lines:** First bearish, second bullish: second closes at the same level as first close.
**Bearish Meeting Lines:** First bullish, second bearish: second closes at the same level as first close.

**Psychology:**
The second candle gapped in the opposite direction but then reversed to meet at the prior close — shows the gap was a trap.

**Trust percentage:** ~68%.

---

### 20. Bullish Counterattack Lines

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
A bearish candle followed by a bullish candle that opens lower but closes at the same level as the previous close.

**Psychology:**
The gap down was completely reversed; buyers fought back to exactly where sellers started.

**Trust percentage:** ~65%.

---

### 21. Bearish Counterattack Lines

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
A bullish candle followed by a bearish candle that opens higher but closes at the same level as the previous close.

**Psychology:**
The gap up was completely reversed; sellers fought back to exactly where buyers started.

**Trust percentage:** ~65%.

---

### 22. Thrusting Lines

**Type:** Bullish (but weaker)

**How it works:**
A bearish candle followed by a bullish candle that opens lower and closes into the prior body, but NOT past the midpoint (unlike Piercing Line).

**Psychology:**
Buyers tried to reverse but didn't manage to push past halfway. Weaker bullish signal than Piercing Line.

**Trust percentage:** ~55% (unreliable alone).

### 23. On-Neck Pattern

**How it works:**
A bearish candle followed by a smaller bearish candle that opens lower but closes near the prior close.

**Psychology:**
The gap down was supported briefly then stabilized. Bearish signal, but weak.

**Trust percentage:** ~50% (needs heavy confirmation).

### 24. In-Neck Pattern

**Type:** Bearish (weak)

A bearish candle followed by a bullish candle that opens lower but closes at the prior close level.

A failed recovery attempt. Bears remain in control.

## C. THREE-CANDLE PATTERNS

---

### 25. Morning Star

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
Three candles: a long bearish, a short indecision candle (doji or spinning top), and a long bullish that closes well into the first bearish candle.

**Identification:**
- Prior downtrend
- 1st: long bearish
- 2nd: small-bodied candle (doji or spinning top) — gaps down from 1st (ideally)
- 3rd: long bullish — gaps up or closes well into the 1st body

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Day 1: strong selling
- Day 2: the sell-off stalls — indecision, no follow-through
- Day 3: buyers completely take over
- This three-day structure shows the full transition from bearish → neutral → bullish

**Psychology:**
- **Day 1:** Bears dominate (despair)
- **Day 2:** "Why isn't it dropping more?" — uncertainty; the aggressive sellers have finished
- **Day 3:** "It's going back up!" — relief, buying pressure, trapped shorts cover

**Trust percentage:** ~82%. One of the most reliable reversal patterns.

---

### 26. Evening Star

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
A long bullish candle, a small indecision candle (gapping up ideally), and a long bearish candle that closes well into the first bullish body.

**Identification:**
- Prior uptrend
- 1st: long bullish
- 2nd: small body (doji/spinning top), ideally gaps up
- 3rd: long bearish, ideally gaps down or closes well into 1st body

**Psychology:**
- **Day 1:** Euphoria
- **Day 2:** The rally pauses — "nobody knows what to do"
- **Day 3:** Reality check; all the late buyers from day 2 are trapped; panic selling

**Trust percentage:** ~82%.

---

### 27. Morning Doji Star

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
Same as Morning Star, but the middle candle is specifically a doji (not any small body).

**Why it is believed to work:**
A doji in the middle shows even more complete indecision than a spinning top. Slightly stronger signal.

**Trust percentage:** ~84%.

---

### 28. Evening Doji Star

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
Same as Evening Star, but the middle candle is a doji.

**Trust percentage:** ~84%.

---

### 29. Three White Soldiers (Three Advancing White Soldiers)

**Type:** Bullish Reversal / Continuation

**How it works:**
Three consecutive long bullish candles, each closing higher than the previous, with each body near the high of each session. Each opens within the previous body.

**Identification:**
- Appears after a downtrend (reversal) or consolidation
- Three long white candles
- Each closes progressively higher
- Small or no upper shadows
- Each opens within the prior candle's body (not above the high)

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Consistent, controlled buying over three sessions
- No profit-taking — each day opens lower (within prior body) and rallies further
- Shows persistent, measured accumulation

**Psychology:**
- **Day 1:** First buyers step in — "maybe the bottom is in"
- **Day 2:** "I missed yesterday, let me buy the open" — more buyers
- **Day 3:** "This is a real rally" — general recognition; momentum
- Controlled buying, not euphoria — which makes it sustainable

**Trust percentage:** ~80% as reversal. Weaker in overbought conditions (~65%).

---

### 30. Three Black Crows

**Type:** Bearish Reversal / Continuation

**How it works:**
Three consecutive long bearish candles, each closing lower than the previous, each opening within the prior body.

**Psychology:**
- Controlled, persistent distribution
- No bounce — each session opens within prior range and continues lower
- Systematic selling without relief

**Trust percentage:** ~80%. Weaker in oversold conditions.

---

### 31. Three Inside Up (Bullish Tri-Star)

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
A bearish candle, a smaller bullish candle inside it (harami), then a third bullish candle that closes above the first candle's close.

**Psychology:**
- Day 1: selling
- Day 2: the selling stops (inside day)
- Day 3: buyers push above day 1 high — full reversal confirmed

**Trust percentage:** ~72%.

---

### 32. Three Inside Down (Bearish Tri-Star)

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
A bullish candle, a smaller bearish candle inside it, then a third bearish candle below the first.

**Psychology:** Selling follows a pause in buying.

**Trust percentage:** ~72%.

---

### 33. Three Outside Up

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
A bearish candle, a bullish engulfing candle (engulfs the first), then a third bullish candle that closes higher.

**Psychology:**
- Day 2 is the strong reversal (engulfing)
- Day 3 confirms the reversal by continuing higher
- More immediate conviction than Three Inside Up

**Trust percentage:** ~78%.

---

### 34. Three Outside Down

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
A bullish candle, a bearish engulfing candle, then a third bearish closing lower.

**Trust percentage:** ~78%.

---

### 35. Abandoned Baby (Bullish / Bearish)

**Type:** Reversal

**How it works:**
- **Bullish:** A bearish candle, a doji that gaps below both adjacent candles (isolated), then a bullish candle that gaps up
- **Bearish:** A bullish candle, a doji that gaps above both adjacent candles, then a bearish candle that gaps down

**Why it is believed to work:**
- The doji is completely isolated by gaps on both sides — "abandoned"
- Represents a complete break from the prior trend
- Very rare but very powerful

**Psychology:**
- The first gap (into the doji) traps the prevailing trend traders
- The doji represents maximum uncertainty
- The second gap (out of the doji) traps the opposite direction — everyone who traded at the doji level is wrong

**Trust percentage:** ~85-90% when it appears correctly. Very rare but one of the most reliable patterns.

---

### 36. Upside Gap Two Crows

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
In an uptrend: a long bullish candle, a gap-up bearish candle (first crow), then another bearish candle that opens higher but closes lower, within the first crow's body.

**Psychology:**
- Gap up should have been bullish but the first crow closed lower — warning
- Second crow tried to rally further but fell even more within the first crow's range
- Both days after the gap were bearish — classic failed breakout

**Trust percentage:** ~72%.

---

### 37. Downside Gap Two Crows

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
In a downtrend: a bearish candle, a gap-down bullish candle, then a bullish candle that opens lower but closes higher within the first gap candle.

**Trust percentage:** ~70%.

---

### 38. Two Black Gapping

**Type:** Bearish Reversal / Continuation

**How it works:**
Two bearish candles that gap below a prior bullish candle. The second does not close below the first.

**Psychology:**
A double gap-down shows persistent selling with no relief. Both sessions were bearish start to finish.

**Trust percentage:** ~65%.

---

### 39. Three-Line Strike (Bullish / Bearish)

**Type:** Reversal

**How it works:**
**Bullish:** Three long bullish candles followed by one very long bearish candle that closes below the first bullish candle's open. Despite the sell-off, the three prior gains get "struck" in one session.

**Bearish:** Three bearish candles followed by one long bullish candle that closes above the first bearish.

**Psychology:**
Three strong sessions followed by one huge counter-session that wipes out all progress. Shows a sudden overwhelming shift in sentiment.

**Trust percentage:** ~72%. Counter-trend move often fails — the original trend tends to resume.

---

### 40. Advance Block

**Type:** Bearish Warning / Reversal

**How it works:**
Three bullish candles where each subsequent candle has a smaller body, longer upper shadow, and/or closes nearer the high but with less momentum.

**Identification:**
- Three white candles
- Each body is getting smaller
- Upper shadows getting longer
- Third candle may close lower than the second

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Each candle shows diminishing buying pressure
- The longer upper shadows mean sellers are appearing at each new high

**Psychology:**
"The rally is getting tired." Each day, buyers are less enthusiastic and sellers are pushing back more. Classic momentum divergence.

**Trust percentage:** ~70%. Warning signal, not a definitive reversal.

---

### 41. Deliberation

**Type:** Bearish Warning

**How it works:**
Two strong bullish candles followed by a small-bodied third that gaps up but shows no follow-through (spinning top or doji).

**Psychology:**
- Two strong pushes up
- Third day gaps up but can't advance — "we stopped"
- The gap becomes a trap if the next session closes below it

**Trust percentage:** ~68%.

---

### 42. Identical Three Crows

**Type:** Bearish

**How it works:**
Three consecutive bearish candles with identical (or near-identical) closes, each opening lower but closing at the same level.

**Psychology:**
Each day opens lower, but closes at the same level — sellers can push it down intraday but buyers defend a specific level. More of a warning than a continuation.

**Trust percentage:** ~60%.

## D. MULTI-CANDLE / COMPLEX PATTERNS

---

### 43. Tasuki Gap (Bullish / Bearish)

**Type:** Continuation

**How it works:**
**Bullish:** a gap-up in an uptrend, two candles in the gap zone, then a third candle fills the gap and continues up.
**Bearish:** reverse for downtrend.

**Psychology:**
The gap fills (profit-taking) but the trend resumes — the pullback was a trap for bears.

**Trust percentage:** ~72%.

---

### 44. Rising Three Methods

**Type:** Bullish Continuation

**How it works:**
A long bullish candle, followed by three (or more) small bearish candles that trade within the first body (they don't break below), then a final bullish candle that closes above the first.

**Identification:**
- In an uptrend
- 1st: long bullish
- 2nd-4th (or more): small bearish candles inside the first range
- Last: bullish closes above the first candle's close

**Why it is believed to work:**
- The small bearish candles are profit-taking, not distribution
- The range holds above the first candle's low → support holds
- The final bullish candle confirms the trend is intact

**Psychology:**
- **Day 1:** Strong up day
- **Days 2-4:** "Taking a breather" — weak holders take profit; strong holders hold
- **Day 5:** "Break's over, back to work" — new buyers step in; shorts cover
- The pause was just rest, not reversal

**Trust percentage:** ~82%. Very reliable continuation pattern.

---

### 45. Falling Three Methods

**Type:** Bearish Continuation

**How it works:**
A long bearish candle, 3 small bullish candles inside the range, then a final bearish candle below the first.

**Psychology:** Small bounces inside a downtrend are just dead cat bounces. Sellers resume.

**Trust percentage:** ~82%.

---

### 46. Upside Tasuki Gap

**Type:** Continuation

**How it works:**
A gap up, a bullish candle, a bearish candle that opens within the first gap candle and closes below it but above the original gap. The gap partially fills and then the trend continues.

**Trust percentage:** ~72%.

---

### 47. Downside Tasuki Gap

**Type:** Continuation

**How it works:**
A gap down, a bearish candle, a bullish candle that partially fills the gap, then trend resumes down.

**Trust percentage:** ~72%.

---

### 48. Mat Hold (Bullish / Bearish)

**Type:** Continuation

**How it works:**
Similar to Rising Three Methods but the small counter-trend candles break out of the first candle's range but then reverse back.

**Trust percentage:** ~78%.

---

### 49. Ladder Bottom

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
Three or more consecutive long bearish candles (the ladder), followed by a doji or small bullish candle after a gap, then a strong bullish candle.

**Psychology:**
- The market "falls down the ladder" with relentless selling
- Then it stops (doji) and reverses (strong bullish close)
- Capitulation followed by exhaustion and reversal

**Trust percentage:** ~75%.

### 50. Ladder Top (High Wave)

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
Strong rally forming a ladder up, followed by a high-wave candle (long shadows, small body), then a bearish candle.

**Psychology:**
The long shadows show extreme disagreement at the top. The high-wave candle is the turning point.

**Trust percentage:** ~68%.

### 51. Stick Sandwich

**Type:** Bullish Reversal (in downtrend) / Bearish Reversal (in uptrend)

**How it works:**
**Bullish:** Bearish candle, bullish candle, then another bearish candle that closes at the same level as the first bearish. The two bearish candles "sandwich" the bullish.

**Psychology:**
The middle bullish candle tried to reverse but the third bearish brought it back to the same level — bearish continuation initially. However, the fact that it held the same low creates a double-bottom support.

**Trust percentage:** ~65%.

---

### 52. Homing Pigeon (Bullish / Bearish)

**Type:** Reversal (similar to Harami)

**How it works:**
Two bearish candles in a downtrend where the second has a body completely inside the first. Like a harami with both bearish.

**Psychology:** The second bearish candle failed to make a lower close — selling pressure is diminishing.

**Trust percentage:** ~65%.

---

### 53. Concealed Bullish / Bearish Engulfing

**Type:** Continuation

**How it works:**
In an uptrend, a bearish candle is followed by a bullish engulfing candle — but the bullish candle closes higher than the prior bullish candle (not just the prior bearish). This "conceals" the normal engulfing as a reversal and instead signals continuation.

**Trust percentage:** ~72%.

---

### 54. Western Pattern

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
A long bullish candle, followed by a doji that gaps above it, then another bullish candle that gaps above the doji. Third candle gaps up again. But each subsequent candle has diminishing bodies/length. This stresses and exhausts buyers.

**Trust percentage:** ~68%.

---

### 55. Kicking (Bullish / Bearish)

**Type:** Reversal

**How it works:**
**Bullish:** A bearish marubozu followed by a bullish marubozu that gaps above the first.
**Bearish:** A bullish marubozu followed by a bearish marubozu that gaps below.

**Why it is believed to work:**
- Two extreme candles with a gap between them
- The complete reversal of conviction from one extreme to the other
- The gap traps all participants from the first session

**Psychology:**
- **Day 1:** Complete conviction in one direction (marubozu)
- **Gap and Day 2:** Complete conviction in the opposite direction
- Everyone who participated in day 1 is completely trapped
- Emotional whiplash

**Trust percentage:** ~80-85%. Powerful but rare.

---

### 56. Kicking - Senryu Cross Type

**Type:** Reversal

**How it works:**
Same as kicking but the second candle has a cross (doji) instead of a gap. Slightly weaker.

**Trust percentage:** ~75%.

### 57. Separating Lines (Bullish / Bearish)

**Type:** Continuation

**How it works:**
**Bullish:** A bearish candle followed by a bullish candle that opens at the same level as the prior close (the two candles "separate" at the open of day 2).
**Bearish:** Reverse.

**Psychology:**
The second candle opened exactly where the prior one closed — showing clean follow-through. No gap, no hesitation. Precise continuation of intent.

**Trust percentage:** ~70%.

## E. SAKATA'S FIVE METHODS (SASHISEN)

---

### 58. Three Buddha Top (Three Mountains)

**Type:** Bearish Reversal

**How it works:**
Three distinct peaks at similar levels (like a triple top) using candlestick analysis. Each mountain represents a failed attempt to break higher. The three mountains represent greed, hope, and finally despair.

**Psychology:**
- **1st mountain:** Enthusiasm, early buyers
- **2nd mountain:** Hope that the trend continues
- **3rd mountain:** Desperation — the last chance to sell before the collapse

**Trust percentage:** ~78% (similar to triple top).

---

### 59. Three Buddha Bottom (Three Rivers)

**Type:** Bullish Reversal

**How it works:**
Three distinct bottoms (like a triple bottom). Each represents a test of support with diminishing selling pressure.

**Psychology:**
- **1st bottom:** Panic, capitulation
- **2nd bottom:** Doubt — "are we going lower?"
- **3rd bottom:** Resolve — "this is the bottom, I'm buying"
- After the third bottom, the river (market) flows upward

**Trust percentage:** ~78%.

---

### 60. Three Gap Fill (Three Gaps)

**Type:** Exhaustion / Reversal

**How it works:**
Three consecutive gaps in the same direction. The first gap = breakaway, second = measuring/runaway, third = exhaustion. After the third gap, the trend reverses.

**Psychology:**
- **Gap 1:** News-driven, strong conviction
- **Gap 2:** Momentum, FOMO
- **Gap 3:** The last gasp — everyone who could possibly buy/sell has already done so
- No fuel left for the fire

**Trust percentage:** ~70%. The third gap often fills quickly.

---

### 61. Three Parallel Lines (Same Price Levels)

**Type:** Continuation

**How it works:**
Three bullish candles where each close is at roughly the same price (parallel highs). Each session opens higher but runs into selling at the same level. It's a distribution pattern that eventually breaks down.

**Psychology:**
Buyers keep pushing to the same level but can't break through — selling at that level is absorbing all buying. Eventually the buying exhausts.

**Trust percentage:** ~65%.

---

### 62. Three Red / Three White (Alternative to Three Soldiers/Crows)

**Type:** Reversal / Continuation

**Three Red:** Three consecutive red candles — confirmed bearish momentum.
**Three White:** Three consecutive white candles — confirmed bullish momentum.

**Trust percentage:** ~75% for momentum continuations.

> **Important Caveats:**
> - No pattern works 100% of the time. Always use stop-losses.
> - Reliability depends on timeframe (higher timeframes = more reliable).
> - Volume confirmation significantly improves reliability.
> - Context matters: patterns at support/resistance, trendlines, or moving averages are more reliable.
> - Different asset classes have different success rates for the same patterns.
> - These percentages are statistical averages from research — past performance does not guarantee future results.
